LESSON 02

Clarity Over Cleverness

Once you learn that prompts work better when they're specific, there's a temptation to overdo it. To get fancy. To add words like "expertly" and "with deep insight" and "as a world-class authority."

Don't.

The best prompts read like instructions you'd give a smart intern who's never met you. Plain language. Clear nouns. No hidden assumptions.

What "clever" looks like (and why it backfires)

Bad: "Craft a masterful and engaging treatise that artfully
      explores the multifaceted dimensions of remote work."

That sentence has zero useful information in it. "Masterful" — masterful by whose standard? "Engaging" — engaging to whom? "Multifaceted dimensions" means nothing.

Good: "Write a 400-word blog intro about the trade-offs of
       remote work. Audience: small business owners. Tone:
       practical, not preachy. End with a question to the reader."

Notice the second one is longer in word count but actually shorter in fluff. Every word does work.

The "smart intern" test

Read your prompt out loud. Imagine you're handing it to a competent human you just met. If they'd ask follow-up questions, your prompt is missing something.

Pete's rule

When you're tempted to add an adjective, replace it with a sentence of detail instead. "Engaging" → "make the reader want to keep reading by opening with a specific story." That's actionable. "Engaging" isn't.

Hidden assumptions kill more prompts than any other mistake

Watch this:

"Summarize my notes."

What's the AI supposed to summarize? You haven't given it any notes. It'll either ask for them, hallucinate some, or summarize nothing. None of those is what you wanted.

"Summarize the meeting notes I'm pasting below into 5 bullet
points, keeping any decisions and action items.

[paste notes here]"

Now it has the input AND knows what shape the output should take.

Concrete > Abstract

Whenever you have a choice between abstract and concrete, pick concrete:

  • "Make it shorter" → "Cut it to 150 words"
  • "Make it more professional" → "Use formal verbs and remove all contractions"
  • "Improve the structure" → "Add a heading every 3 paragraphs"

The AI can act on the right side. It has to guess at the left side.
