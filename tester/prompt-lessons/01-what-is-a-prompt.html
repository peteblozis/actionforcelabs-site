LESSON 01

What Is a Prompt, Really?

A prompt is just the message you send to an AI. That's it. There's no special syntax, no magic words, no secret format.

But here's the thing most people miss: the AI can only work with what you give it. It doesn't know what's in your head. It doesn't know what you mean. It only knows what you said.

Why phrasing matters

Watch what happens with two near-identical prompts:

Prompt A: "Write something about dogs."
Prompt B: "Write a 200-word friendly intro for a dog-training blog
           aimed at first-time owners."

Prompt A could come back as anything — a sonnet, a Wikipedia summary, a sales pitch. Prompt B comes back as exactly the thing you wanted, on the first try.

The difference isn't that Prompt B is "better at AI." It's that Prompt B answered the four questions Prompt A left open: How long? What tone? What format? Who is reading it?

Key insight

If you're disappointed in an AI's answer, the fix is almost never "use a smarter AI." It's "tell it what you actually want."

The 80% fix

Before you send any prompt, ask yourself one question: "Did I tell it what success looks like?"

If a stranger read your prompt with no context, would they know:

  • What kind of output you want (paragraph, list, table, code)
  • How long it should be
  • Who the audience is
  • What tone fits

If any of those four are missing, the AI is guessing. Sometimes its guess is fine. Often it isn't. Adding even one of those four cuts the back-and-forth dramatically.

Try it

Open your AI tool. Send this:

Help me with my email.

Look at what comes back. Then send this:

Help me write a 3-sentence email to my landlord asking about
the broken dishwasher. Polite but firm. I've already mentioned
it twice.

Notice the difference. That's the whole game.
