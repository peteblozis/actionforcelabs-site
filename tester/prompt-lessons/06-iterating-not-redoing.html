LESSON 06

Iterating, Not Redoing

Beginners get an answer they don't love and start over. They throw away the conversation, write a new prompt, and hope this one works better.

That's almost always the wrong move.

The AI already understands the topic from the conversation so far. Telling it to fix one specific thing is faster, more accurate, and preserves what was working.

The "fix this one thing" pattern

Instead of starting over, just tell it what to change:

"That's close but make it 50% shorter."

"Good — but rewrite the second paragraph in a more
 conversational tone. Keep the first and third as-is."

"The structure works. Replace the example about coffee
 with one about gardening."

"Drop the last sentence. The rest is good."

Each of these is a tiny prompt that produces big improvement, because the AI already has all the context.

The three iteration moves

1. "More" / "Less"

"Make it 30% shorter."
"Add 2 more examples."
"More technical detail in section 2."
"Less salesy throughout."

2. "Replace"

"Replace the 'leverage' with 'use'."
"Swap the intro for a question instead of a statement."
"Use a recipe example instead of a cooking example."

3. "Keep / change"

"Keep the structure, change the tone to friendlier."
"Keep paragraph 1 exactly. Rewrite the rest."
"The list is great. Just renumber so each item starts
 with an action verb."
Why this is faster

Starting over means re-explaining everything. Iterating means saying one sentence. A 30-minute task with restarts becomes a 5-minute task with iterations.

When to actually start over

Two cases:

  • The whole approach is wrong — you got an essay when you wanted a checklist. Iteration won't fix that. Start fresh.
  • The conversation got long and confused — sometimes after 15 back-and-forths the AI starts losing track. New conversation, fresh slate.

Outside those two, iterate.

The "save before you change" habit

Before you ask for a change, copy what you have so far into a notes file. Sometimes the iteration makes things worse, and you want to roll back. Five seconds of saving prevents real frustration.

Try it

Next time you get an AI answer that's close to what you wanted but not quite, resist the urge to rewrite your prompt. Instead, send a one-sentence iteration:

"Same idea, but cut it in half and remove the bullet points."

Notice how much faster you get to the final answer.
