LESSON 05

Constraints and Format

You've been clear about the goal. You've given an example. Now there's one more lever: tell the AI what shape the answer should be.

Length. Structure. Headings or no headings. Lists or paragraphs. JSON or plain text. These are constraints, and they're the difference between an answer you can use and an answer you have to reformat by hand.

The four most useful constraints

1. Length

"Answer in 3 sentences."
"Limit to 150 words."
"Give me a one-paragraph summary."
"Write at least 500 words."

Word counts and sentence counts are the easiest constraints to enforce. AIs respect them about 90% of the time.

2. Structure

"Use these sections:
1. Problem
2. Cause
3. Fix
4. Prevention"

If you know the shape you want, name the sections. The AI will fill them in. This is enormously faster than getting a blob of text and then reorganizing it yourself.

3. Format

"Return as a markdown table with columns:
 Name | Price | Availability"

"Return as JSON with keys: title, summary, tags (array)."

"Use bullet points, no paragraphs, max 7 bullets."

Specifying format saves you from copy-paste-clean-up. Especially valuable for any answer you'll feed into another tool.

4. What NOT to include

"No introduction. No conclusion. Skip the disclaimer.
 Just give me the steps."

Sometimes the most useful constraint is what to leave out. AIs love adding "I hope this helps!" and "It's important to note that..." Tell them not to.

Combining constraints

You can stack them:

"Summarize the article in:
 - 3 bullet points
 - Each bullet 1 sentence
 - First word of each bullet must be a verb
 - No introduction, no conclusion"

Stacking 3-4 constraints is fine. Stacking 10 starts to confuse the AI and quality drops.

The friction principle

Every constraint you add up front saves you reformatting time on the back end. Five seconds of "make it a markdown table" beats two minutes of converting paragraphs into a table by hand.

Format request templates worth saving

These three are worth memorizing:

For meetings:
"Format as: Decisions made, Action items (with owners),
 Open questions."

For research:
"Format as: TL;DR (1 sentence), Key facts (5 bullets),
 What I'm uncertain about, Sources."

For drafts:
"Format as: Subject line, Body (under 150 words), P.S.
 (optional, only if it adds value)."

Building a small library of format templates is a multiplier. Lesson 08 covers exactly how to organize this.
