LESSON 04

Show, Don't Tell — Examples

If you remember nothing else from this series, remember this: examples are the highest-leverage thing you can put in a prompt.

Telling an AI "make it casual" is fuzzy. Showing it one casual sentence and saying "match this" is exact.

Why examples beat instructions

Instructions describe the destination. Examples show it. The AI doesn't have to interpret what "casual" means to you specifically — it has a sample.

Instructions only:
"Write a friendly product description, not too formal,
 with a bit of personality."

Instructions + example:
"Write a product description in this voice:
 'This mug doesn't care if it's Monday. It just wants to be
 full of coffee and sit in your hand. 12 oz. Dishwasher safe.'

 Now write one for: a wool blanket"

The second prompt nails the tone every time. The first gets you something different on every run.

The "before and after" example pattern

This is the most powerful pattern in prompting. Instead of describing what to do, show the input and the desired output:

"Rewrite customer feedback into bug tickets.

Example:
INPUT:  'the search is broken when i type my name'
OUTPUT: 'BUG: User search returns no results when query
         contains user's own name.'

Now do these:
1. 'login page just spins forever'
2. 'cant upload more than 3 files'
3. 'dark mode keeps flipping back to light'"

The AI learned the pattern from one example. It applies it consistently to all three.

How many examples do you need?

One good example is usually enough. Two is great. Three is the upper limit before diminishing returns.

What matters more than quantity is quality:

  • The example should be representative, not edge-case
  • The example should match the difficulty of what you're really asking for
  • The example should be correct (the AI will mimic mistakes too)
Common trap

Don't use only easy examples and then ask for a hard one. If your examples are simple sentences but the real task is complex paragraphs, the AI will assume "short and simple" is the rule. Match example difficulty to actual difficulty.

When examples don't help

If you can't write a good example yourself, examples won't save you. The AI can't infer what you want better than you can articulate it. In that case, go back to Lesson 02 and get clearer on the goal first.

Try it

Pick something you've struggled to get the AI to do consistently. Write one example of the answer you wished you'd gotten. Put it in your next prompt. Watch what happens.
